# FACTS — sources for every number in the film

The film uses one **illustrative worked example**, not a measurement of any real stall. Figures vary widely by city, rent, price and footfall.

| Claim in film | Value used | Source and wording |
|---|---|---|
| Price of a cup | ₹15 (100 ml) | OkCredit, "How to Start a Tea Stall Business in India in 2026": worked example "a 100 ml cup sold at ₹15" — https://okcredit.in/blog/how-to-start-tea-stall-business-in-india |
| Variable cost per cup | ≈ ₹6.60 (milk ≈ ₹3.70, tea ≈ ₹0.83, sugar ≈ ₹0.47, rest cup + fuel) | Same OkCredit page: "about ₹6.60 in variable cost for a 100 ml cup sold at ₹15", milk "about ₹3.70, tea ₹0.83 and sugar ₹0.47", using Department of Consumer Affairs average retail prices (30 Oct 2026) |
| Left per cup | ≈ ₹8.40 | Same page: "leaves ₹8.40 toward rent, wages, repairs and your own pay" |
| Fixed monthly costs | ≈ ₹25,000 (rent, helper, electricity) | Same page: illustrative break-even of about 100 cups a day against roughly ₹25,000 in monthly fixed costs; owner's own labour not included |
| Daily cups (base) | 200 | Planning range: IIFL example 150 cups/day at ₹15 — https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/how-to-start-a-tea-stall-business-in-delhi-ncr ; realistic single stall 100–400 cups/day (summary of sources in search) |
| Daily cups (busy) | 400 | Upper end of the same range; Bihar example of a stall selling "around 400 cups of tea every day" (calendar-uk FAQ citing reports) |
| Monthly at 200 cups | 200 × 8.40 × 30 = ₹50,400 → "करीब ₹50,000"; − ₹25,000 = ₹25,400 → "लगभग ₹25,000" | Arithmetic |
| Monthly at 400 cups | 400 × 8.40 × 30 = ₹1,00,800 − ₹25,000 = ₹75,800 → "करीब ₹75,000" | Arithmetic; assumes the same fixed costs (a busier spot can have higher rent) |

Other reported figures (not used as claims in the film): StartupTalky's estimate of ₹3,000–5,000/day for a busy roadside stall; OkCredit's ₹40,000–1,00,000/month for a good stall; self-reported chain outlets claiming lakhs per month (unverified).
