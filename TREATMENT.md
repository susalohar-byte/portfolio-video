# कितना कमाते हैं एक चाय स्टॉल के मालिक? — Treatment

Style: **Mid-century Cartoon** (`midcentury-toon`) · 1080×1920 (9:16, Shorts/Reels; same delivery format as the user's previous film) · 24 fps · ~60 s · Hindi voice (edge-tts `hi-IN-MadhurNeural`, free) · Hindi subtitles burned in + `.srt`.

All money figures are an **illustrative worked example** from public 2026 sources (FACTS.md), said as "करीब / लगभग" and labelled "अनुमानित उदाहरण" on screen. No real stall, person or brand is shown.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The cup, opened (chosen).** A ₹15 cup is literally taken apart into its cost layers like a 1950s appliance-manual exploded view, then the leftover ₹8.40 is multiplied by cups/day → month → minus fixed costs → take-home, then a busier variant. A "recipe/ritual card" grammar: step → quantity → result.
2. **A day on the clock.** Mantel clock from 5 a.m. to 10 p.m.; every hour drops cups into a jar; evening count. Strong for rhythm, weak at explaining *margin*, which is the real insight.
3. **Do / don't split.** "Price is what matters" vs "Count is what matters" in two colour blocks. Clear twist but nothing to *see being done*; too abstract for a held-body, hands-at-work style.

Chosen: **1**, because the style's native move is "the hand *is* the step" and the round call-out is the close-up. Every number lands on an object the hand touches. Opening and ending come from the topic: the film opens on the price itself (₹15 stamped on a steaming cup) and ends on the viewer's own neighbourhood stall (a question card, held dead still).

## 2. Logline and arc

Logline: *A ₹15 cup of chai is opened up layer by layer to show what really reaches the chaiwala's pocket, and why the count of cups matters more than the price.*

- **Setup (0–17 s):** hook price → question → the cup is opened, three ingredient call-outs, the total cost.
- **Turn (17–41 s):** ₹8.40 per cup looks tiny; multiplied by 200 cups and 30 days it becomes ₹50,000, then fixed costs take half → ≈₹25,000. Same stall, 400 cups → ≈₹75,000.
- **Ending (41–60 s):** the truth line ("not the price, the count"), then a still question card for the comments.

## 3. Benchmark

- **1950s consumer-economics classroom shorts** (grammar only): one sentence per picture, numbered cards, direct address, calm narrator. *Learn:* pacing of one fact per beat, medallions that open the next idea. *Don't take:* any character, lettering, music, footage.
- **Modern "how much does X earn" Shorts**: *Learn:* hook with the price in the first 2 s, running total that the viewer can follow, CTA question at the end. *Don't take:* their faces, brands, thumbnails, sensational claims or unsourced numbers.

## 6. Cue map

Cool-jazz combo, **112 bpm** (beat 0.536 s), 4/4. Vibraphone chord per step (each cost layer its own chord, brought back one per item in the month montage).
- Intro (0–8): brushes + walking pizz bass, flute pickup into the stamp on "पंद्रह".
- Cup opened (8–17): half-time feel, vibraphone chord on each call-out (C maj7 / A min7 / D min7 / G7).
- Split + multiply (17–27.5): double-time brushes, celesta arpeggio climbing as the grid fills, finger snaps on every row.
- Costs (27.5–33.3): bass alone + bongos, a low vibe chord per bite; **full cut to silence 33.3–33.8** before the result.
- Result (33.8–37.5): vibraphone glissando + sustained maj9.
- Busy corner (37.5–43.5): up a whole step (D), sticks on ride.
- Truth + CTA (43.5–60): back to C, sparse flute, room tone alone under the still card, final vibe chord on iris-out.
Cuts, stamps and pops land on beats; voice lines start ~0.2 s before their iris (J-cut).
